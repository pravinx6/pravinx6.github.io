Do Marks Really Measure a Student's Ability?

Personal reflection · Student life · Education

In my opinion, marks can show how a student performed in an exam, but they cannot completely measure a student's ability. I have felt this personally. Sometimes I study for the same amount of time as another student and prepare the same topics, but they score higher than me. It doesn't always mean that they are more capable. Sometimes they are simply better at handling exam pressure and expressing what they know.

I have experienced situations where I studied a topic and knew that I had learned it, but when I saw the question in the exam hall, I suddenly felt like I knew nothing. Because of the pressure and fear of the exam, I couldn't remember the answer. After leaving the exam hall, when the pressure was gone, I could understand the answer just by looking at it. That made me feel that I actually knew the topic, but I couldn't show my knowledge at that particular moment.

At the same time, I don't think marks are completely meaningless. A student who gets high marks usually has put effort into studying and deserves appreciation for that. But I don't think those marks should make us believe that the student is better than everyone else. Another student might spend time developing coding skills, communication, creativity, problem-solving or other practical abilities and may not get the same marks.

I also feel that constantly ranking students can affect their confidence. When the highest-scoring student is repeatedly praised while the students at the bottom are ignored, some students may start believing that they have no talent. They may even stop trying new things because they think someone else will always be better than them.

I don't think we should completely remove grading. Instead, I believe our education system should give more importance to practical learning. For example, instead of only reading a coding topic from a textbook or watching a PPT, students should actually write the code, understand how it works and use it to solve real problems. Learning through real-world activities and practical experiences can show abilities that marks alone cannot.

For me, marks are a measure of exam performance, not a complete measure of a person's ability. A student who scores less is not necessarily less talented. Sometimes they simply haven't found the right way to show what they are capable of.
